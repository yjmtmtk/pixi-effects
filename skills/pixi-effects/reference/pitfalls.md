# pixi-effects pitfalls — what actually went wrong

Collected by having fresh AI sessions build pieces from the docs alone: first six (kinetic type, news lower-third, slideshow, 2.5D showcase, three.js title, bar chart), then a portfolio of 16 more (items 32 and up), made by three different models. Each entry is a real stumble. **Status**: `fixed` = the library now handles or warns; `docs` = nothing can catch it, so remember it; `hand-roll` = the DSL has no feature, see the workaround.

A good habit that catches most of these: after `init`, read `window.__logs` (see `template.html`) — every pixi-effects warning says what to change.

## Time

1. **Keyframe `at` starts at 0 when THIS layer appears** *(fixed in 0.3.0; earlier versions measured from the composition and failed silently)*. A layer at `at: 2` with a keyframe `at: 0` animates at global 2 s. Negative `at` counts back from the layer's end. A keyframe that starts after its layer ends now warns ("never plays").
2. **A layer's own `at` and a transition's `at` are composition time** (seconds from the parent composition's start). Only keyframes are layer-local.
3. **Layers are hidden, not removed, after `[at, at+duration)`** and keep their last values. Z-order is array order (later = on top). To switch scenes, stack scenes with `at`/`duration`, each starting with an opaque full-screen rect. *(docs)*
4. **Transitions need overlapping lifespans.** Both layers must be alive for the whole window: scene `i` starts at `i·(L−T)`, the transition's `at` is the next scene's start, total = `n·(L−T)+T`. A scene that ends while the transition is still running leaves a gap. *(docs)*
5. **Sound effects need no file at all:** `{ type: 'audio', sfx: 'pop', at }` — the layer lasts exactly as long as the sound. **Audio length of a FILE.** Omit `duration` on a one-shot clip: the layer lasts exactly as long as the file (nothing to measure; the only sample file is `examples/_assets/bgm.mp3`, 6 s). `loop: true` with no `duration` lasts until the composition ends. An explicit `duration` longer than the clip goes silent after the clip unless `loop: true` (`bgm.mp3` is 6 s) and warns. Volume fades work like any keyframe: `volume: 0` + `{ at: 0, to: { volume: v }, duration: 2 }` + `{ at: -2, to: { volume: 0 }, duration: 2 }` (the last 2 s only; `set` is a jump). *(fixed: volume keyframes used to ramp from the previous keyframe's end, so the music faded across the whole piece)*
6. **Snap times to the frame grid (`Math.round(t*30)/30`) when two things must stay locked** (a bar and its label). Drive both from one JS easing function with per-frame `set` keyframes rather than mixing a tween with computed positions. *(docs)*
7. **Pick eases on purpose.** `expo.in` stays tiny until the very end (a "flood" circle never reaches the corners — use a bigger radius and `power2.in`). A zoom transition with `power2.out` looks like a cut because the incoming scene is nearly opaque after 40 %. Default is linear. *(docs)*
8. **`render()` runs in roughly real time** (6 s of 720p ≈ 6 s). Listen to `movie.on('progress', e => e.progress)` (0–100). *(docs)*

## Layout and anchors

9. **Defaults differ by type.** rect/circle/ellipse are centred on `x,y`; text and image are top-left; a composition has no anchor — use `pivotX/pivotY` and set `x,y` to where the pivot should land. For a bar growing from its base use `anchorY: 1` (or `anchorX: 0`). *(docs)*
10. **Shape geometry may be at the top level or in `initial`** (`width height radius anchorX …`). *(fixed: `initial` used to be silently ignored)*
11. **Angles are degrees** — `rotation`, `skew*`, `rotationX/Y` — and `rotation: -0.2` is almost no rotation. *(docs)*
12. **Text**: anchor 0.5 centres the line *box*, not the cap height (an underline under serif or italic text needs ~`0.9·size` below the centre: `0.46·size` touches the descenders of a g or y). Arial Black is ~0.8·size wide per glyph, monospace ~0.6·size; leave ~7 % for hold-scale. `style` accepts any PixiJS TextStyle field: `letterSpacing`, `wordWrap` + `wordWrapWidth`, `lineHeight`, `stroke`, `dropShadow`, `padding`. A glow (`dropShadow` with `blur`) is clipped unless `padding ≥ 2·blur`. *(docs)*
13. **`w` / `h` in expressions** are the layer's own size. For text they now describe the styled text (`x: '-w'` scrolls a marquee fully out). *(fixed)*
14. **Masks live in the parent's coordinate space and do not follow the masked layer.** Reveal by growing the mask's `width` from a fixed edge (`anchorX: 0`); exit by tweening `x` and `width` together; slide the content separately. *(docs)*
15. **`line` / `polygon` / `path` use plain canvas coordinates** when you give no `x,y` (they used to be drawn around (0,0)); with `x,y` those place the shape's centre. Stroke colour and width go in `initial`. A background pill must be sized from its text (~0.58 × fontSize per glyph + padding): `inspect` does not check text against shapes. *(fixed)*
15b. **`filterArea` is in the layer's own coordinates** (origin = its local origin, e.g. a circle's centre), not the parent's. A blurred shape is clipped to its bounding box without it: `filterArea: { x: -(r+260), y: -(r+260), width: 2*(r+260), height: 2*(r+260) }`. `threeD` layers pad automatically. *(docs)*
16. **The player bar overlays the bottom ~60 px of the canvas** in browser screenshots (not in the exported video). `movie.snapshot()` / `movie.contactSheet()` never include it; keep captions above it anyway. *(fixed: use the built-in snapshot tools)*

## Missing features (hand-roll)

17. **Gradients / vignettes**: use shape `fillGradient` (linear or radial, stops may have alpha). Earlier sessions hand-rolled canvas images or dozens of stacked bands. *(fixed)*
18. **Counters**: `text: '{value}'` + animate `value` (with `format`). Other text content cannot change over time. Earlier sessions generated one text layer per number (40+ per bar). *(fixed)*
19. **Loops**: `repeat` (finite) / `yoyo` / `repeatDelay` on any keyframe; infinite repeats are rejected with a warning. *(fixed)*
20. **No per-frame expression time.** Expressions are evaluated once at build. A camera orbit is `orbit()`; a shake or drift is `wiggle()`, travel along a path is `followPath()`, and any other curve (waves, spirals) must be sampled into short linear keyframes (~0.1 s) in a JS loop. **Randomness must be seeded** (`rand(seed)` / `noise(x, seed)` in expressions, `random(seed)` in JS): `Math.random()` makes playback and the exported file disagree. *(partly fixed)*
21. **Per-letter / per-word text is `animateText()` (one call: a layer per piece, a wave in and out) or `splitText()` by hand (one layer per piece, positions measured for you); a group / parent is a `{ type: 'null' }` layer + `parent: 'name'`, no caption-linked-to-slide.** One layer per letter; many layers; seeded random loops (`random(seed)`). *(hand-roll)*
22. **Colour tweens look muddy in RGB** (red→green passes through brown). Use `colorSpace: 'oklab'` or `'oklch'` on the layer. In `oklch`, a nearly grey end (chroma < ~0.08, e.g. `#9a9ab4`) borrows the other end's hue, so grey → yellow no longer flashes through pink. *(docs / fixed)*
23. **Generated images**: `canvas.toDataURL()` as an asset `src` works. Make them ≥ canvas size or `kenBurns` zooms look soft. *(docs)*

## 2.5D / three.js

24. **`threeD` is required** for `z`, `rotationX`, `rotationY` (they warn otherwise). Camera props go in `initial`/keyframes, never on the camera object. `+z` is toward the viewer. *(fixed: warns)*
25. **A layer at/behind the camera plane is hidden** — the default camera sits at `z = (H/2)/tan(fov/2)` ≈ 989 at 720p, fov 40 (≈ 808 at fov 48, lower at higher fov). Keep every layer's `z` below it. *(fixed: warns once)* **It is hidden whole when ANY corner of it passes the camera plane**: a big plane tilted with `rotationX` / `rotationY` (a ground plane, an orbit ring) vanishes as soon as one corner swings behind the camera (the warning then says a corner reaches the plane). Tilt it less, shrink it, or keep the camera clear of its depth range. *(docs / fixed: warns)*
26. **Under a dolly zoom only the `z = 0` plane stays put**; everything nearer balloons and clips. Headline at `z: 0`, near cards `z ≤ ~70`. Perspective also pulls far layers toward the centre: put far cards at the outer x, near cards inward, and keep ~10 % margin for an orbit sweep. `orbit({ dollyZoom: { from, to } })` does both together. *(docs)*
27. **Combining orbit and dolly zoom**: `orbit()` specifies `z`, so it stops following `fov` — write your own `fov` and `z = (H/2)/tan(fov/2)` keyframes for the zoom. *(docs)*
28. **Nearer `threeD` layers sort on top of text** — lay out around them; equal `z` keeps array order. A card (rect + icon + label that move together) is a `composition` with `threeD: true`. A final call-to-action or overlay should be a plain 2D layer (last in the array): it ignores the camera, and fade the 3D scene and headline out before it appears (a dim rect alone leaves them visible). *(docs)*
29. **three.js**: the layer clips at its own rectangle — size it to the area you want and pull the camera back (`z ≈ 5.4` for a radius-1 torus knot at fov 50). Metal needs an environment map (`PMREMGenerator(ctx.renderer)`); `three/addons` is not in the importmap. Route `three` through the importmap so there is one copy. Masks/transitions/`filterArea` are not supported on `threeD` layers. *(docs)*

## Tooling (agent harness)

30. **A scene change must keep the outgoing scene alive until the covering flood / wipe has finished**, and the next scene starts when it is complete; the flood circle must reach the corners (radius ≥ half the diagonal) and use `power2.in`, not `expo.in`. *(docs)*
30b. **Unknown option names now warn** (`contactSheet({ cols: 2 })` → did you mean `columns`; `init({ fps })` → `frameRate`). Layer names show up in `inspect` paths, so give layers a `name`. `inspect` ignores faint (alpha < 0.3) layers and, while a geometry keyframe (x y z scale rotation …) is running on the layer, its `null` parent or its composition, a text crossing the edge on purpose (a marquee, a zoom-through); a fade, a `set` and an x written only in `initial` are no motion, so a cut-off title is reported once it has settled. Pixi may print `[BindGroup] … destroyed while still bound` warnings at `movie.destroy()`: harmless (playback and seeking never warn). *(docs)*
30c. **Look with the built-in tools, not OS screenshots**: `await movie.contactSheet({ count: 6, as: 'dataURL' })` returns one labelled image of the whole animation; `await movie.inspect(frame)` returns every layer's canvas bounds and `issues`. If you do use a browser tool, `screenshot` needs an **absolute path**, macOS `sed -i` needs `''`, and a static server may redirect `/x.html` → `/x` and drop `?query`. Keep the template's `try/catch` around `init` so failures land in `window.__logs`. Check ≥ 5 timestamps including mid-transition. *(fixed: tools built in)*
31. **Check audio with `movie.inspectAudio()`**: every sound measured on its own (`sound.length`, `peakDb`, `loudestAt`, `brightnessHz`), when it plays, loudness and brightness per 0.1 s window, and `issues` (the mix was limited, a layer is inaudible, an sfx is cut off by the end). Decoding a render by hand is no longer needed: `render()` encodes the same mix, on time in mp4 / mov / webm / mkv. *(fixed)*

31b. **A mistyped `ease` now warns** (`ease: 'power3.outt'` → did you mean `power3.out`). It used to run silently as the default ease (`power1.out`), so the motion changed and nothing said so.
31c. **`draft` and `scale` make the file smaller, not the drawing faster** (measured: a 48 s 1080p film exports in about the same time as a draft; the file is 7× smaller). To save time, export a part (`range` / `--range` / `--scene`), look at single moments (`check --no-export --at …`), and keep motion blur off while you iterate.

## Round 2 — found by building 16 portfolio pieces (examples/gallery)

32. **Seeking is safe now** *(fixed)*: a `set` keyframe (counter value, text fill, image tint, shape style) is undone when you seek back before it, and shapes are redrawn before they are culled, so a rect growing from `width: 0` at the left edge draws after any jump seek (contact sheet, snapshot, scrubbing, export). The old workarounds (`width: 0.01`, a one-frame `to` instead of `set`) are not needed.
33. **A mask starts and ends with the layer it masks** *(fixed)*, so its keyframes count from that layer's start. A mask with an explicit `at` keeps composition time. Before, a mask `at: 0` reveal had finished before a layer that appeared at 8 s, and the layer stayed invisible without any warning.
34. **`from` and `from+to` show their start value from the LAYER's start**, not from the keyframe's `at` (as in GSAP and After Effects): a delayed fade-in `{ at: 2, from: { alpha: 0 }, to: { alpha: 1 } }` needs no `initial.alpha`. To keep the current value until `at` and then jump, use `set`. *(docs)*
35. **One `Movie` per page.** Creating a second Movie (for an isolated repro) and destroying it breaks the first (`null.clear`). Reload instead. *(docs)*
36. **Multi-line text is centre-aligned by default** (`style.align: 'left'` for left-aligned paragraphs). *(docs)*
37. **Composition size.** A 2D composition does not clip its children (a child outside `width × height` still draws), but its rectangle is the culling area: if the whole rectangle is off the canvas the group is skipped even when a child is on screen — keep it near what it draws, or canvas-sized. A `threeD` composition (a card) IS clipped at its `width × height` (it is rendered into a texture that size): size it for the soft shadow too. A growing `threeD` shape or text is fine: its texture is resized in place (it used to warn "destroyed while still bound"). *(measured; docs / fixed)*
38. **Under an orbit the side the camera swings toward becomes the NEAR side**: a "far" card at `z: −200` can be drawn bigger than 1× on that side and run off the frame (`inspect` only checks text). Pull those cards inward / lower `z`, and check a frame at the extreme of the sweep. After `orbit()` ends the default camera returns, so to hold the final pose add a camera layer that starts there. Near particles balloon under a dolly zoom: cap particle `z` at about +150, or one becomes a fake full stop. *(docs)*
39. **`fillGradient` stops span the whole unmasked shape**, not the visible part: under a mask, place the stops for the visible fraction (e.g. 0.64→1). A translucent shape over near-black reads as grey: put the alpha in the gradient stops. *(docs)*
40. **Rotate a polygon / path / line about a chosen point with `pivotX` / `pivotY`** (in the shape's own canvas coordinates; put the same point in `x, y`): `{ points, initial: { pivotX: 600, pivotY: 300, x: 600, y: 300, rotation: 180 } }` turns about the vertex (600, 300) — measured. Without a pivot the shape turns about the centre of its bounds. `anchorX` / `anchorY` do nothing on these shapes (they are for rect / circle / ellipse). *(docs; the gallery wrapped every polygon in a composition unnecessarily)*
41. **Glow a whole set of layers** by putting them in one `composition` with a blur filter and `filterArea` (only a composition carries a filter for its children): wide enough for the blur, and the set is built once, so the extra copy is just a second group. *(docs)*
42. **Transition looks:** the default `dissolve` reads as a hard black-and-white camouflage and `scale: 220` as static; `scale: 90, smoothing: 0.5` is a soft film dissolve. The default edge softness of `iris` (0.02) and `wipe` blurs flat graphics at 720p: `smoothing: 0.004`–`0.01` gives a clean edge. *(docs)*
43. **Layers built in a loop need their z-order planned**: anything pushed to the array after the loop is drawn over what the loop made (rings crossed over a header). Keyframes on different properties of one layer may overlap in time. Keep a subject in the upper ~60 % of a source image if a caption will sit under it (`inspect` does not look at picture content). *(docs)*
44. **Zero-pad a counter with `format: { pad: 2 }`** (`18:42:{value}` shows `18:42:05`). A `{value}` placeholder that nothing sets or animates prints 0 and now warns: `{value}` is reserved, there is no way to print it literally. *(fixed)*
45. **The same mistake in a loop warns once**: the warning "keyframe starts after the layer ends" is summarised after three layers. The usual cause is an off-by-one (`f <= frames` instead of `f < frames`) that puts the last sample exactly at the layer's end. *(fixed)*
46. **Still not in the DSL (hand-roll):** `zIndex` (stack order is list order). *(Earlier round-two notes listed draw-on strokes, changing text, jitter and particle emitters here: they are built in now: `trimEnd`, `set: { text }` / `visibleChars`, `wiggle()`, `particles()`.)*
46b. **Blend modes exist now** (`blendMode`: `add` `screen` `multiply` and, since 0.22, 15 more CSS names (pitfalls 78–80), on any layer, threeD layers and compositions included; a composition's children inherit it). Additive white cores no longer read grey on a coloured background. It also works on a layer with `filters` (a blurred glow with `add` / `multiply`): the mode is applied by the last filter, because a container's blend inside a filter pass multiplied against nothing and rendered black. With `add`, use dim saturated colours: overlaps sum, and near-white sums to pure white. *(fixed)*
47. **Browser tooling: prefer `npx pixi-effects check page.html`** — it runs its own private Chrome, so none of the following can bite. If you drive a browser by hand instead: the `agent-browser` daemon is shared, so a `contactSheet` can come back from another session: after saving an image, look at it and confirm it is YOUR piece. In zsh a variable holding `agent-browser --session x` is not split into words (use a small wrapper script). `inspect` now clips overlaps to the visible area, ignores the two scenes of a running transition, treats text inside a moving (panned) composition as moving, and does not report text scaled to 0 as "no size"; it still does not compare text with picture content or shapes.
48. **A composition's `alpha` is applied to each child separately, not to the flattened group** (measured: two overlapping opaque circles in a composition at `alpha: 0.5` show the lower one through the upper one in the overlap). Fading a group that has overlapping children therefore shows the overlap; fade the children themselves, or keep overlapping parts out of the group. Expressions have no way to read a sibling's size: measure text with `canvas.measureText` (the font string Pixi uses) and compute pill widths in JS. *(docs)*
49. **sfx timing and level.** An sfx layer's `duration` is the sound's length (it stretches, it does not trim). Align the loud point: a `riser` peaks at its end, a `swoosh` 0.16 s in. Many sounds at the same instant add up: above 0.9 the mix is limited and a warning names the layers to turn down. Preset names, knobs and voice fields are closed lists — a guess (`whoosh`, `ding`, `impact`, `sawtooth`, `lp`, `decay`) warns with the right word. *(fixed: warns)*
50. **An image (or video) works as a mask, inverted too** *(fixed)*: PIXI hides a sprite mask with `renderable = false` and the lifespan toggles used to switch it back on, so the mask picture was drawn over the layer (a white disc) and an inverted mask looked ignored. Use the picture's alpha (or red channel) as the shape: an opaque word or logo on a transparent canvas image is a text-shaped mask with real glyph edges. A `text` layer also works as a mask (cut to its glyphs; measured with a heavy sans `I`). *(docs / fixed)*
51. **Arcs and rings are `shape: 'arc'`** (`radius`, `innerRadius`, `startAngle`, `endAngle`, degrees; 0° = 3 o'clock, clockwise). A progress ring is an arc with `startAngle: -90` and an animated `endAngle` (−90 → 270); `strokeCap: 'round'` rounds BOTH ends. Fill it for a pie slice, add `innerRadius` for a donut slice (a 120-polygon donut is no longer needed). Style (`strokeColor`, `strokeWidth`, `fillColor`) goes in `initial`; `strokeCap` / `strokeJoin` may be top level. *(fixed)*
52. **Measure and split text with `measureText` / `splitText`, not by hand.** `splitText(text, style, { by, x, y, align })` returns each piece's x / y / width in the renderer's own font metrics (kerning kept; verified: a whole line vs the same line in per-letter layers differs only by anti-aliasing). The pieces are for text layers with the default top-left anchor and the same `style`. Load a web font before measuring (`await document.fonts.load('24px "Name"')`) or the fallback font's widths are used. Size a pill from `measureText(...).width + padding` instead of `0.58 × fontSize × length`. *(fixed)*
53. **Draw-on and typewriter need no masks or per-letter layers.** `trimStart` / `trimEnd` (0–1 of the outline, animatable, every shape but `arc`) draw a stroke on: `trimEnd: 0` plus a keyframe `to: { trimEnd: 1 }`. The fill is not trimmed, so fade `fillAlpha` in afterwards; use `strokeCap: 'round'`. Text changes over time with `set: { text }` (swap at a time) and `visibleChars` (typewriter; left-align it so it grows rightward). A `to: { text }` cannot tween a string and warns. *(fixed)*
54. **A deck page lasts until its last stop plus the transition.** `deck()` lays pages out one after another in page-local time and a `transition` overlaps them: a page shorter than its last stop (or shorter than the transition) loses its final step or flashes. A step is a layer that starts at the previous stop (`at: <previous stop>`), so the thing that appears on a press is fully visible when the movie stops. The transition option is `kind`, not `type`. If the last stop is mid-exit on paper, flag the stop you want in the PDF: `{ at, pdf: true }` (in `deck()`: `stops: [1.2, { at: 3, pdf: true }]`), and `pdf: false` keeps one out.
55. **Presenter and Controller both listen to click and keys.** Use the `Presenter` for a talk (or let the Controller's Present button start one); do not run both on the same canvas yourself. `movie.play()` ignores stops, but the Controller's play button / Space / click-on-picture play to the next stop and pause (`pauseAtStops: false` plays through); `next(movie)` (from `pixi-effects/present`) plays to the next stop (a second press skips to it), `prev(movie)` jumps back (nothing plays in reverse).
56. **Analyse audio before `init`, and give it the movie's frame rate.** `const env = await audioEnvelope('music.mp3', { frameRate })` is async; `react()` is pure and bakes the keyframes, so there is no per-frame callback. A custom `bands` list replaces the defaults (include `bass` if you want it; beats are picked there). No file yet: `bpmEnvelope(120, { duration })` is a stand-in with a kick on every beat. Offset the music with `audioOffset` when the audio layer does not start at 0.
57. **Review a deck by its stops, not by its timeline.** `pixi-effects check` writes `stops.png` (one picture per stop, in order) for a page with `stops`; the picture at a stop is what the audience reads while it waits. A stop that lands a few frames before an animation ends shows it half-finished (also in the PDF): `inspectStops(movie)` / the check's review list names such stops; give an animation a little more time than its step (e.g. the next stop 0.3 s after it ends).
58. **`music` counts BEATS, and every track has to add up.** A token's `:n` is beats (a quarter note), a 4/4 bar is 4: a bar of `c4:1 e4:1 g4:1` is a beat short and every later bar drifts. Keep all tracks the same length (the music lasts the longest; a track of rests `'_:16'` sets a length) and check the printed length with `movie.inspectAudio()`. 6/8 is a bar of 3 beats (dotted quarter `:1.5`, eighth `:0.5`) with `grid: 2`; `grid: 3` is triplet eighths in 4/4.
59. **A groove that must enter late needs `from`, not a long string.** `drums: [{ kick: 'x...' }, { from: 8, snare: '....x...' }]`. Patterns repeat; a drum object is a loop of its longest string. A `crash` on the last bar: its own section `{ from: 28, crash: 'x' }`.
60. **Build-ups are layers and `vol` points, not louder notes.** A track's `vol` can be `[[0, 0], [8, 0.7]]` (a fade over 8 beats); the pad's `attack` can be 2–3 s; enter instruments with rests. Per-note `!` and `,` only accent and soften.
61. **Music is a bed: `volume` 0.5–0.8 under speech or sfx.** At `volume: 1` it peaks at −6 dBFS (an sfx peaks at −12). A layer shorter than the music cuts it with a fade; longer is silent after it unless `loop: true`. Do not also add an mp3 bed.
62. **Chord names are voiced close around octave 3**: use `[c3 g3 e4]` for an exact voicing or `Am7/e` for a bass note; `swing` only moves notes that start on an off-beat eighth (`.5`) or sixteenth (`.75`); the bell and music box ring for `ring` seconds whatever the written length (shorten `ring` for fast bell lines); the synth is a handy bed, not an orchestra: keep tracks to 3–6, vary the bars, and end on the tonic.
63. **`animateText()` / `splitText()`: `x` and `y` are pixel numbers, and `y` is the TOP of the line.** An expression such as `x: 'GW/2'` throws (the letters are measured once, at build time): write `x: W / 2`. The pieces are centred half a line below `y`, so a line drawn under the text goes at `y + lineHeight` (about 1.2 × `fontSize`), not at `y`; look at the contact sheet, the layout check cannot see an underline through a word. (`check` writes into `check-out/` of the current folder unless you pass `--out`.)
64. **`animateText()`: `out: { preset, at: T }` starts the exit at composition second T; without `at` the exit is the end of the layer run backwards.** `duration` is still how long until the layer ends (every piece is gone by `at + duration`): with `out.at`, give `duration` room for the exit and the wave after T (a warning says `out.at ≤ …` when it does not fit, or when T is before the entrance has finished). Without `out.at`, to be gone at time T set `duration: T − at` (title at 0.4 s that must be gone by 8.0 s: `duration: 7.6`). A hand-written exit keyframe works the other way: a negative `at` counts back from the end of its own layer, a positive one from the start of its own layer, never from the movie.
65. **A spring that does not spring, and `'auto'` that is not a spring.** `spring(1,170,26)` is critically damped (damping ratio 1): it never overshoots; at stiffness 170 use damping below 20 (`spring(1, 170, 12)`), or pick a preset (`spring.bouncy`, `spring.wobbly`). With a number `duration`, that number is the settle time (mass and stiffness only matter with `duration: 'auto'`). `duration: 'auto'` needs a spring ease (otherwise it warns and uses 0.5 s) and is for keyframes, not for `animateText` / `orbit` tweens. A colour that overshoots is clamped in its colour space; `alpha` past 1 shows nothing.
66. **Animating a gradient: one `fillGradient` object, partial, in the keyframe.** `to: { fillGradient: { angle: 360, stops: [...] } }` (keys you leave out stay). Not `'fillGradient.angle'`, not `gradientAngle`, not `fillGradient: 'red'` (each warns once with what to write). The layer must start with a `fillGradient`; `stops` keep their count; `type` cannot change. A fade between two colours in the default `rgb` passes through grey (green → magenta): set `colorSpace: 'oklch'`. Under a mask the stops still span the whole shape (see 39).
67. **A key the layer does not have is warned about now (it used to be ignored in silence).** `{ type: 'text', anchorX: 0.5 }` (anchors go in `initial`), `{ type: 'audio', sfx: 'click', pitch: 3 }` (the knob goes inside: `sfx: { preset: 'click', pitch: 3 }`), `{ type: 'shape', shape: 'rect', radius: 5 }` (that is a circle's key; a rect has `cornerRadius`), a mistyped property in a keyframe (`alhpa`), a mistyped text `style` key. The warning names the key, the kind of layer and what was probably meant; fix the key, do not silence the warning. Three gallery pieces had the `pitch` mistake for months: their sound effects were never pitched.
68. **A pitched sfx keeps its own length; a radial gradient is placed in the shape's bounds.** `sfx: { preset: 'chime', pitch: -5 }` lasts as long as the sound does, so near the end of the movie give the layer a `duration` that ends with it (`inspectAudio()` reports a sound cut off by the end). A radial `fillGradient`'s `center` / `radius` are fractions of the SHAPE, not the canvas: for a glow on a big circle, convert canvas pixels to fractions of the circle. `music.reverb` is 0–0.6.
69. **Inside a remapped composition, `at` is in THAT composition's seconds.** With `speed: 0.5` a 4 s composition shows only 2 s of its content; a layer at `at: 3` inside is never seen (warns: "its clock only reaches 0–2s"). With `speed: 2` the content is 8 s long, so children default to 8 s and `at: 5` is fine. The composition's own `at`, `duration`, x / alpha keyframes are outer time. To freeze, hold `time` at one value (`to` the same number); to rewind, `to: { time: 0 }`. A sound inside goes silent while the clock stands still, and an `sfx` longer than the clock's reach is reported as cut off by the clock: give it a shorter `duration` (the sfx limit is 10 s) or let the clock run on. A child whose life ends exactly where a held `time` stands is hidden during the hold: give it a `duration` that reaches past (the content is as long as the larger of `duration` and the largest `time`). **With `time` keyframes the composition's own `duration` is its life in OUTER time and must reach the last keyframe** (`keyframes[1] starts at 2.3s, after the layer ends` means `duration` is too short): `at` of a `time` keyframe counts from the composition's start, the content length is a separate thing that comes out of the `time` values.
70. **`speed` is a setting, `time` is the property.** `speed: 2` on the layer; to change the speed over time animate `time` (a curve of seconds in the file), never `keyframes: [{ to: { speed: 3 } }]` (warns). `speed: 0` is not allowed (hold a moment with `time` to the same value twice); `speed` together with keyframed `time`: `time` wins. Other names (`playbackRate`, `timeScale`, `rate`, `reverse: true`, `timeRemap`) are warned about with what to write. `particles({ speed })` is something else: launch speed in px per second.
71. **Sound follows `speed` like a tape: the pitch moves with it.** An audio file or a video's sound at `speed: 2` is an octave up; backward plays reversed. Not for `sfx` / `music` layers (warns): change an sfx with `pitch` (semitones), or put the sfx **inside a remapped composition** and it follows that clock (slower and lower, or backward).
72. **A video at `speed: 2` with no `duration` is half as long; with a `duration` longer than the file it holds the last frame** (add `loop: true` to repeat). A negative `time` counts back from the end of the file (`initial: { time: -2 }` = two seconds before the end).
73. **Know the shape of the curve before you time a beat to it.** `cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1)` does half of its change in the first 13 % of the time and 99 % by 74 % (not slow motion: over 6 s the first 2 s of content pass in 0.6 s); `.2, .8, .5, 1` has a shorter tail. `power2` is a cubic (`power1` = t², `power3` = t⁴): `power2.in` has done 12.5 % at half time and 42 % at three quarters, so a rewind with it dismantles most of the title in the last third.
74. **`focus` as a layer name means that layer's FIRST `z`, and the name must be unique.** (Two layers with one name: the first `threeD` one is used and it warns.) The camera does not follow a layer whose `z` moves (it warns): to follow it, animate `focus` itself with numbers. A name no layer has, or a layer that is not `threeD`, warns and the `z = 0` plane is used (did-you-mean for typos).
75. **Depth of field blurs `threeD` layers only, and the layer's own depth decides it.** A 2D layer never blurs; a `threeD` layer at the focus depth stays sharp; the depth is measured along the camera's view direction, so with an orbiting or tilted camera a layer off to the side of the look-at point is a little deeper than its `z` says and blurs a little even at the focus `z`; one blur for the whole layer, so a tilted floor is one blur (split it into layers).
76. **`aperture` is the lens diameter in px, not a 0–1 strength.** 30 is the default (clearly shallow), 0 turns it off, 60 is strong, 100 is extreme (small bright objects speckle); the blur never exceeds 32 px. 20 or more blurred layers at once warns (one filter pass each, more on WebGPU).
77. **A dolly does not need the camera's home distance typed in:** keep `z` automatic and animate `offsetZ` (negative = closer). Writing `z` yourself means typing `H / 2 / tan(fov / 2)` (about 989 at 720p, fov 40; as an expression `H / 2 / tan(20 * PI / 180)`). A bokeh field is a few big cards, not dozens of layers: a card at depth `z` fills the frame at size `(W / s) × (H / s)`, `s = camZ / (camZ − z)`.
78. **A blend mode mixes the layer with everything BELOW it, in the order of the array.** The backdrop goes first, the blended layer after it; a layer placed before its backdrop blends with nothing. Names are CSS names with the hyphen: `soft-light`, not `softLight` (the warning says which you meant).
79. **On a plain `composition` a `blendMode` makes each child blend by itself, not the composition as one picture.** To blend the whole group give the composition `threeD: true` (z 0, default camera: nothing else changes) or a filter. A text, image or video layer as the (not inverted) `mask` cannot carry a blend (it warns, draws normal); a shape mask and an inverted mask work.
80. **`soft-light` / `overlay` do almost nothing over near-black or near-white; `color-dodge` over dark gives a saturated colour, not white; `blendMode` cannot be keyframed** (use a second short-lived layer). **The 14 new modes cost about 2 ms a layer on WebGL; 10 or more on screen at once warns.** A glow, a haze or a colour cast is ONE layer with a gradient (`fillGradient`), not dozens of circles; keep `add` / `screen` / `multiply` for big piles of particles.
81. **A layer named in `mask` is a matte: it is not drawn, and it must be alive whenever the layers it cuts are.** `{ name: 'band', … }` plus `mask: 'band'` on the text hides the band itself; the matte's time is the composition's time (its own `at` / `duration`). Where the matte is missing the layers it cuts are invisible: the build warns when its lifespan does not cover its users. To show the band as well, add a second layer that looks the same.
82. **A `mask` name works inside one composition only.** The matte and the layers that name it are siblings in the same `sequences`. A layer inside a nested composition cannot name a matte outside it (warns, with the closest name if there is one, and the layer is drawn without the matte); a layer that a transition wraps keeps its matte. Put the matte inside the nested composition, or the layer next to it.
83. **`channel: 'luma'` reads brightness, `'alpha'` (the default) reads opacity.** A white shape is 1 for both, a black shape is 0 for luma and 1 for alpha, a grey `#808080` is 0.5 luma. Outside the matte layer's own shape both are 0, so a wide gradient rectangle slid across the layers is a soft moving reveal. Use `luma` with a grayscale image or a gradient layer; use the default for shapes and text. `luminance`, `brightness` and `grayscale` are not channel names: they warn ("did you mean luma?") and alpha is used.
84. **Subtract with `invert: true` in a list; a list multiplies.** `mask: ['panel', { layer: 'hole', invert: true }]` is "inside `panel`, outside `hole`". A mask written in place inverts the same way: `mask: { layer: { type: 'shape', … }, invert: true }` (the old `maskInverted: true` key is gone: it warns and says this). A luma wipe's `map` is a built-in name (`linear` `diagonal` `radial`) or a grayscale image asset; an unknown name warns once and wipes as if the map were white.
85. **One light layer lights every `threeD` layer, and what no light reaches is black.** Add `{ type: 'light', kind: 'ambient', initial: { intensity: 0.2 } }` (a white ambient light at 1 is the same as no light). A layer you want flat-lit is `lit: false`. Layers without `threeD` are never lit, and a light only counts while it is alive (give it the scene's `at` / `duration`): with no light alive the layers are drawn unlit.
86. **A shadow needs `castsShadows: true` on the light AND on the layer.** Either alone shows nothing (the build says which half is missing). The caster has to be a `threeD` layer in front of the receiver (between it and the light); a receiver takes at most 4 casters. A hard shadow can look like a dark slab: `shadowDiffusion: 10`–`20` softens it, and soft shadows cost the most, so use them on one or two lights. A spot's pool on the wall has radius about `distance × tan(coneAngle / 2)` and can hide behind the card that stands in front of it.
87. **The light's numbers go in `initial` / `keyframes`; `kind`, `falloff` and `castsShadows` are on the layer.** `{ type: 'light', intensity: 1 }` warns; so do `target` / `pointOfInterest` (use `lookAtX / lookAtY / lookAtZ`), `angle` (`coneAngle`), `castShadows` (`castsShadows`) and `intensity: 80` (it is a multiplier, 1 = full, not a percentage). A spot needs a look-at point (it defaults to the middle of the picture).
88. **A room is layers in distance order, not geometry.** There is no depth buffer: a floor or wall is a big `threeD` layer whose origin is at its far edge (`anchorY: 0` / a `z` behind what stands on it), and planes that cross cannot be drawn. Each composition has its own lights: a light in a nested composition lights only that composition's layers. Fog is the camera's (`fogNear` / `fogFar` / `fogColor`), fades `threeD` layers only, so draw the background in the fog colour.
89. **A shader layer is written like Shadertoy, in GLSL ES 3.00: `mainImage` and `fragColor`.** Not `void main()`, `gl_FragColor`, `texture2D`, `varying`, `#version` or a `uniform float iTime;` (each warns with what to write). `iTime` is the layer's own seconds from its start (not the movie's), `fragCoord` starts at the bottom left, the colour is opaque unless `transparent: true`, and the layer cannot read what is behind it (it is a layer, not a filter): there is no `iChannel0`, `iMouse` or `iDate` in this version.
90. **`uniforms` are declared for you; keyframes name them `'uniforms.speed'`, and vectors and colours by component `'uniforms.tint.0'`.** `uniforms: { tint: '#ff8040' }` is a `vec3` of 0..1, so animate its channels, not the string. Declare every uniform you animate with a starting value (a keyframe on a name that is not there warns with the likely name). Do not write `uniform float speed;` yourself.
91. **A shader that does not compile is a magenta checkerboard, and the warning has your line numbers.** Fix the line it names. Loops need a constant bound (`for (int i = 0; i < 8; i++)`): a `while`, or a bound that is a variable, can hang the GPU and warns. A heavy shader is cheaper with `resolution: 0.5`; many small shader layers cost more than one big one. A mask or matte cuts what the shader drew, it does not move the pattern: put the pattern's centre (a `vec2` uniform) where the matte is. A shader layer with a small `width` / `height` sits by its top-left corner at `initial.x / y`. With `resolution` below 1 the shader's own pixels are fewer: write positions as `fragCoord / iResolution.xy`, or multiply pixel uniforms by `resolution`. A shader can itself be a mask (`mask: { type: 'shader', transparent: true, fragment }`: its alpha is the mask).
92. **`warp` is for bending a layer's picture, a `shader` layer is for drawing something new.** `warp` takes `strength` in px (a ripple is 4 to 10, over 200 is refused) and `scale` in px; `speed: 0` stands still. It follows the time of the frame by itself: do not keyframe a time. Its options animate by the filter's name (`'filters.ripple.strength'`). For a swirl or a lens use `twist` or `bulgePinch`.
93. **A `threeD` layer's shadow budget is four casters per receiver, counted in layer order, not geometry.** Five letters as five `castsShadows` layers over one floor: the fifth casts nothing (the warning names it). Make the word ONE text layer (the light is per pixel, each letter is still lit on its own) or give only the four that matter `castsShadows`. Splitting the floor does not help: each half takes the first four.
94. **`resolution` below 1 softens crisp lines in a shader.** `fwidth` anti-aliased edges come out a little blurry after the upscale: keep `resolution: 1` for line art and use 0.5 only for soft fields (flowing colour, glow).
95. **A `color-dodge` leak over a dark picture is a muddy red disc.** Dodge divides each channel of near-black separately, red first. Cut the dodge to the bright subject with a matte (`mask: 'letters'`, a matte-only copy of the word) and put the warm haze in a separate `screen` radial.
96. **A filter option that is a point (`rgbSplit.red`, `glitch` offsets) is tweened one leaf at a time** (`'filters.s.red.x'`; the whole point as a value switches, it does not tween), and a scalar too: `set: { 'filters.tear.seed': n }` steps `glitch.seed` (a `set` keyframe works on filter options; no warning, visible tearing). To switch between looks on one subject, put the subject in time-windowed `composition` copies, each carrying the filters of its look.
97. **A deck inside your own composition loses its `transitions` and `stops`** unless you take them across: `const d = deck({ width: 1920, height: 1080, pages })`, then `{ type: 'composition', sequences: d.composition.sequences, transitions: d.composition.transitions }`. Pass `width` / `height` yourself for a page that is not the movie's own size.
98. **A matte layer needs a fill.** A named matte (`mask: 'glass'`) that is a shape with no `fillColor` / `fillGradient` (or a text with no fill) cuts everything away and nothing warns: the layers it should show simply vanish. Give the matte a fill (white for `alpha`, a gradient for `luma`).
99. **In a `music` score a capital letter is a chord and a lowercase one a note.** `G7` is G dominant seventh, `C5` a power chord, `C6` a sixth chord; `g7`, `c5`, `c6` are notes (octave 7 is a very high note: it used to be what `G7` meant, silently). A bare note in capitals (`Bb2`) is refused with the lowercase spelling (`bb2`); inside brackets (`[C4 E4 G4]`) any case is a note. An unknown chord kind suggests the nearest (`Cmin7` → `Cm7`, `Csus` → `Csus4`, `CM7` → `Cmaj7`).
100. **Read the score back before you trust it.** `check` prints a `music` block: every track's length in bars (a track that ends early or has a short bar is named), the names that start in each bar with their beat (`Am(2)` starts on beat 2; no mark = the downbeat; the first four names of a bar are shown, `musicEvents()` lists everything), and the drum span. Count bars by the read-back, not by hand: a bar of 3 beats in a score with no `meter` is shown as bars of 4, so set `meter: 3` for a waltz. Drums repeat by themselves and `from` / `to` are beats; `swing` only moves notes that start off the beat, so a comp on beats 2 and 4 stays straight.
101. **A film built from one adjective looks like every other one.** Read `reference/direction.md` and write `brief.md` / `style-guide.md` / `shotlist.md` first: real assets with paths, the one sentence to remember, a row per beat with entry and exit state. If a real asset is missing, ask: a drawn fake (a logo, a dashboard, a figure) is the failure that matters. Judge the contact sheet cold: three defects, each with a timestamp, evidence and a local fix.
102. **A vertical cut is a second composition, not a crop, and only when someone asked for it.** One source, a layout function, `?f=9x16`; `check` and `render` take `--query f=9x16` (recipe `two-formats`). Look at a contact sheet of each format: type that fits wide is tiny tall. Ask which formats are wanted; make one if the answer is open. In a 1080p film keep every line of text at 34 px or more; if you cannot read it in a sheet tile, a phone cannot.
103. **Load a web font BEFORE you measure with it.** `measureText` / `splitText` / `animateText` measure once, when the composition is built. Measured in Chrome: "Respect the Local, Loop the Value." at 80 px Lato 900 is 1487 px wide before the Google font has arrived (the fallback) and 1231 px after; Japanese text in a similar fallback was within 3 px. Every box, line break and centred position built from the wrong width stays wrong. `await document.fonts.load('900 80px "Lato"', theTextYouUse)` first (the text argument loads exactly the glyphs you need), then build the composition.
104. **A variable moves values, not timing, and it is read when the composition is built.** `at` and `duration` are plain numbers; `vars: { shift }` works in `x: 'W/2 + shift'` and `'{title}'` inside any string, not in `at: 'shift'`. `setVars` builds everything again (about 20 ms, plus the sound only if it changed): for a continuous input call it as often as you like, calls that overlap are joined, but do not use it for something that should move every frame (that is a keyframe).
105. **Layout that follows the screen is written in `W` / `H` / `GW` / `GH`, never in pixels.** After `movie.resize(w, h)` an expression sees the new size; a literal `x: 960` stays at 960. A string variable cannot be read in an expression (`x: 'title'` is a number context): use `{title}` in a string, or declare the number. A name that is declared but never read, and a `{name}` that was not declared, are both said at `init` (usually a typo). Load a web font BEFORE the first `setVars` that changes a text (see 103): the text is measured again each rebuild.
106. **A text outline used to spike; it is round-joined now.** `style: { stroke: { color: '#000', width: 12 } }` was drawn with PixiJS's default `miter` join, so the sharp corners of W, V, A, k and 4 grew spikes ("the outline sticks out"). The library now adds `join: 'round'` to an outline that names no join; say `join: 'miter'` or `'bevel'` if you want one. The stroke is centred on the glyph edge, so half of it eats into the letter: keep `width` to about a tenth of `fontSize`, or for a thick outline put a second copy of the text with the stroke UNDER the plain one.
107. **`spans` places pieces by measuring, so the font must be loaded first, and a span cannot change size.** A web font that is not loaded yet is measured as the fallback font and the pieces drift apart: `await document.fonts.load('30px "Name"')` before `movie.init`. `fontSize` / `fontFamily` / `lineHeight` / `align` on a span are ignored (warned): they belong to the layer's `style`. `fill`, `text` and `value` keyframes do not reach a spans layer (it is a group of text layers).
108. **Japanese and Chinese do not wrap at spaces** (Korean has spaces, so `wordWrap` breaks there). `wordWrap: true` alone does nothing for a long line (it runs off the canvas); `breakWords: true` breaks between any two characters and can leave one to three characters alone on the last line. Break the line yourself with `\n` after a comma or a particle and size the font to fit; `check` reports a line cut by the canvas edge but does not judge an ugly break, so look at the contact sheet. Raising `fontSize` can turn a clean line into an orphan: look again after any size change.
109. **No font is bundled, and each script needs a font that has its characters.** The machine's own fonts are used; characters a font lacks are drawn with whatever the machine falls back to, so the same page can look different on another machine. Load a web font that covers the script (Noto Sans JP, Noto Sans KR for Hangul, Noto Sans SC) with `await document.fonts.load('40px "Noto Sans KR"')` before `movie.init`, and say it in `style.fontFamily` (pitfall 103). `check` lists a layer none of whose fonts is available, but a family that nothing installs or loads is silently replaced by the next one in the list (or the generic name) without a word: the first name in `fontFamily` is not proof it was used.
110. **Right-to-left text: three traps.** `splitText` / `animateText` order words left to right, so Arabic or Hebrew comes out reversed (split by words yourself, from the right edge, with `measureText` of the logical prefix); a mark below the font box can be clipped (the dots of an Arabic ي) unless the layer has `style.padding`; `check` sees neither. `bulgePinch.center` is 0–1 of the layer, not pixels (pitfalls elsewhere say canvas pixels for the other centres: `twist`, `zoomBlur`, `shockwave`). `npx pixi-effects help arabic` and `help bulgePinch` print the sections.
111. **Only some named filters are tested on both backends.** `glow`, `outline`, `dropShadow`, `bevel`, `bloom`, `pixelate`, `crt`, `rgbSplit`, `dot`, `radialBlur` and `glitch` look the same on WebGPU (the default) and WebGL; `multiColorReplace` draws everything black on WebGPU, `kawaseBlur` is far weaker and `advancedBloom` sharper there: use `colorReplace`, `blur`, `bloom`. A filter follows the edges of the layer's own picture: `glow` on a full-screen opaque layer shows nothing (put it on the shapes and the text).
112. **Text inside a parent that is scaled up comes out soft.** Text is drawn once at its own size and then stretched, so `{ type: 'null', initial: { scale: 3 } }` with a text child of `fontSize: 30` is blurry (shapes stay sharp). Give the text `resolution: 3` (it is drawn at 3 times the pixels, for a parent that grows to 3x; 1 to 8, the cost is the memory), or write it at the size it is shown (`fontSize: 90`, parent scale 1), or let the parent only scale DOWN (a pull-back from big to normal is sharp the whole way). `check --at … --crop` shows the difference.
113. **Two limits on sound that `help sfx` / `help music` did not say.** An `sfx` lasts at most 10 s (`MAX_LENGTH` in the synth): for a longer rain or room tone, chain two audio layers that overlap by a moment, or use `loop` on a short one. A `music` layer plays at most 16 tracks, and a warning says that only the first 16 play: put the rest in a second audio layer.
114. **A keyframe's `from` is on screen from the start of the layer, and it beats `initial`.** `initial: { alpha: 0 }` plus `{ at: 2, from: { alpha: 1 }, to: { alpha: 0.5 }, duration: 1 }` shows the layer at full alpha from second 0 (measured in Chrome: the red square was there at 0.5 s). That is how a title waits invisible with `from: { alpha: 0 }` until its `at`, but it surprises when `from` is the visible end. Give the layer its own `at: 2`, or use `set` at the moment it should appear. No warning: the same pattern (`initial` = the resting value, `from` = where it starts) is what the recipes do everywhere.
115. **A `canvas` layer is a function of the time, nothing more.** The canvas is cleared before every call and nothing survives into the next, so `x += 1` per call, "draw over the last picture" and `Math.random()` all break: a seek or an export would not match playback (`check` draws each canvas layer twice at a few moments and reports a layer whose pictures differ). Compute from `t` (a trail is the positions of the past), use `info.random()` (seeded) and `params` that keyframes animate, and compute a whole simulation in `setup` (an array by frame) and read `info.frame`. A cache of a pure function is fine (same input, same output); state that depends on which frames were drawn before is not. A canvas layer cannot see or paint on what is below it: use `filters`, a matte or a composition for that. Pixel art needs `smoothing: false` (hard pixels when scaled up) and a small `width` / `height` with a big `initial.scale`.
116. **Several canvas layers that need the same thing: module scope and `info.time`, not `setup` and a hand-added `at`.** The state a layer's `setup` returns is private to that layer. A plant, a city or a palette that two layers draw is computed once before `movie.init` (from `random(seed)` and plain functions) and read by both; the shared clock is `info.time` (the movie's seconds), not `t + 3` with the layer's `at` copied in, which goes wrong without a word when someone moves the layer.
117. **A sprite or mask made in `setup` with `document.createElement('canvas')` can be drawn differently the first time.** A GPU canvas used as the source of `drawImage`, under `destination-in` after many anti-aliased strokes, drew up to 128 of 255 differently over 40 000 pixels of a 1280×720 layer the first time and the same every time after. `info.canvas(w, h)` makes a canvas that does not do this; `check` throws away one draw of each moment before it compares, so it does not blame the layer, but an export that draws a frame for the first time is still safer with `info.canvas`. A mask is also easier to get right as: the words first, then `globalCompositeOperation = 'source-atop'` and the texture (recipe `canvas-text-mask`); two `fillText` under `destination-in` intersect.
118. **`check` does not see text drawn in a canvas.** The layout test (cut-off, off-canvas, overlap) reads the text layers; a label or a caption you draw with `fillText` can run off the edge or collide with another without a word, and "no cut-off text" only speaks for the text layers. Look at the sheet and at `--at … --crop`, and keep a margin.
119. **`bitrate` is a ceiling, not a target: a quiet or mostly black picture is written far below it, and no setting changes that.** Asking for `'150M'` gave 105 to 151 Mbps on grainy dark pictures and 13 to 18 Mbps on clean ones (a platform that wants at least 35 Mbps for 4K then rejects the file). The encoder spends bits on what changes: a constant bitrate mode reaches the encoder (it was measured, with H.264, HEVC and VP9) and still writes 0.1 Mbps for a black frame with a circle, because no encoder pads. So read the real rate of the file (`render` prints it and says when it is far under what you asked; `npx pixi-effects probe file.mp4` too) and, when a minimum is needed, raise it by what the picture really has: fine film grain (a seeded noise of about ±2/255 in the darks, which also hides banding in dark gradients) costs bits and looks right. Do not stretch it with an invisible pattern.
